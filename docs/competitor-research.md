## Competitor #1: Fever

### What They Are

Fever is the world's largest event discovery and ticketing platform, operating in 40+ countries with 200M+ users. They serve as a marketplace connecting consumers with curated local experiences — concerts, immersive exhibits, food experiences, wellness events, and nightlife.

### How They Make Money

1. **Commission on ticket sales** — The core revenue engine. Every event booked through Fever generates a cut.
2. **Fever Originals** — They create their OWN events based on data (e.g., Candlelight Concerts, immersive art shows). These are high-margin because Fever controls production, promotion, and ticketing.
3. **Secret Media Network** — A portfolio of city-specific social media pages (Secret NYC, Secret London, Secret LA) that function as "what's going on this weekend" content channels. These pages drive massive traffic back to Fever's marketplace. Most consumers don't realize the media pages and marketplace are the same company.
4. **Promoted listings** — Event organizers pay for premium visibility in the discovery feed.
5. **Brand partnerships** — Sponsorships on Fever Originals and co-branded experiences.

### What They Do Well

**Data-driven content creation.** Fever doesn't just list events — they analyze user behavior to identify demand gaps, then CREATE events to fill them. Candlelight Concerts were born from seeing an uptick in elevated date-night and concert purchases post-pandemic. This flywheel (data → original content → more data) is their moat.

**The Secret Media Network is genius.** They built a media empire of city-specific Instagram/TikTok pages that look like independent editorial content ("10 Things to Do in LA This Weekend") but funnel directly to Fever's marketplace. This gives them an organic distribution channel that competitors don't have.

**Social-first experience design.** Every Fever Original is engineered for shareability — glow-in-the-dark art, candlelit venues, Insta-friendly backdrops. They understand that the event IS the marketing for the next event.

**Full-funnel analytics for organizers.** AI-powered insights, conversion tracking, audience behavior analysis. Serious business tools.

**Acquisitions accelerate their moat.** They bought DICE (music ticketing, June 2025) and Atom Tickets (cinema, March 2025), expanding their category coverage without building from scratch.

### Where They Fall Short (Clubd's Opportunity)

- **No real social graph.** You can "follow friends" on Fever, but it's an afterthought. There's no "Sarah is going to this" social proof driving discovery. It's still fundamentally a search/browse marketplace.
- **Skews toward entertainment, not community.** Fever is great for finding a Candlelight concert or immersive Van Gogh exhibit. It's NOT where you find your local run club, a dad walk group, or a popup yoga session. The vibe is "what should I do this weekend?" not "what community should I join?"
- **Transactional, not relational.** You buy a ticket, attend, leave. There's no ongoing connection to the host, other attendees, or the community around that activity. Every event is a standalone transaction.
- **No host community tools.** Fever is built for large-scale event producers, not the yoga instructor running weekly classes from a park who needs attendee management, reminders, and a way to grow their following.
- **Algorithmic, not social.** Discovery is driven by data models and editorial curation, not by your friends' activity. This works at scale but misses the "I saw my friend went to this" organic discovery that actually drives community event attendance.

### Key Takeaway for Clubd

Fever proves the market for event discovery is massive and monetizable. But they've built a ticketing marketplace for entertainment, not a social platform for community. Clubd can own the space Fever doesn't serve: recurring lifestyle events where the community IS the product, not just the entertainment.

## Competitor #2: Sweatpals

### What They Are

Sweatpals is a social fitness and wellness event discovery platform that lets users find and host fitness events — run clubs, yoga sessions, HIIT workouts, hiking groups, breathwork, pilates, bouldering clubs, and more. Founded in 2021, they've raised $20.3M (backed by a16z speedrun, Patron, Kevin Hart's HartBeat Ventures, and Instacart co-founder Max Mullen).

### How They Make Money

1. **Ticketing commissions** — Platform fee on paid events and classes.
2. **Host subscription tools** — Digital ticketing, waiver signing, participant management, group communication.
3. **Klarna payment plans** — Attendees split high-ticket events into 4 installments; organizers receive full payment upfront. Sweatpals takes a cut.
4. **Membership management** — Hosts can sell ongoing memberships through the platform.

### What They Do Well

**Laser focus on fitness/wellness vertical.** While other platforms try to be everything, Sweatpals owns the "fitness events near me" space. Run clubs, yoga, pilates, hiking, breathwork — all the categories Clubd cares about.

**Riding a massive cultural wave.** Run clubs grew 216% YoY between Jan–Sep 2025 on their platform, with 237,000+ people joining Sweatpals-affiliated run clubs. The wellness-as-social-life trend is real and growing.

**Social + fitness journal hybrid.** Users can journal and share fitness experiences, track progress, and build real friendships through movement. It's "social media that makes you healthier" — a compelling positioning.

**Strong host tools.** Digital ticketing, waiver signing (huge for fitness), participant management, group chat, Klarna integration. They understand what fitness hosts actually need.

**Right cities, right demo.** Top cities: Miami, Austin, Chicago, New York, LA. Top categories: mixed social sports, strength training, pilates, running, hiking. This is Clubd's exact target market.

### Where They Fall Short (Clubd's Opportunity)

- **Too narrow: fitness only.** Sweatpals doesn't cover restaurant popup dinners, craft beer events, art walks, community volunteer days, seal releases, dad groups, matcha meetups, live music in the park, or any of the non-fitness lifestyle events that Clubd wants to serve. Their brand identity is "fitness" — it would feel off-brand to list a cookbook night or a pottery class.
- **No friend-driven social discovery.** Sweatpals has social features (profiles, communities, journaling) but doesn't have the "Sarah is going to this → you discover it" social graph that Clubd is building. Discovery is still category/location-based browse, not socially-driven.
- **Limited brand/sponsor ecosystem.** No marketplace for Lululemon to sponsor a run club or for a local coffee shop to partner with a yoga event. The monetization is purely transactional (tickets/memberships), not ecosystem-driven.
- **Small scale, early stage.** Only 40K active users as of 2023, $1M revenue in 2024. They're growing fast but haven't proven marketplace economics at scale yet.
- **No marketing services for hosts.** No "agency-in-a-box" offering. Hosts still need to figure out their own promotion outside the platform.
- **No "what's happening this weekend" breadth.** If you want to see ALL the cool things happening in OC this Saturday — the run club, the popup dinner, the beach cleanup, the brewery event — Sweatpals only shows you the fitness stuff.

### Key Takeaway for Clubd

Sweatpals validates that the fitness/wellness events vertical is hot and fundable. But they've boxed themselves into fitness-only. Clubd can capture the same wellness-oriented audience while being the platform for ALL lifestyle events — the run club AND the restaurant event AND the community cleanup AND the matcha meetup. Broader catalog + social discovery = bigger TAM and stickier user behavior.

## Notable Mention: Partiful

While not a direct competitor (they focus on private, invite-only events), Partiful is worth studying for their social mechanics:

- **$140M valuation**, 500K MAU, 400% YoY growth, Google's Best App of 2024
- **Genius UX:** Text-based invites (no app download required), social RSVP visibility, playful Gen Z aesthetic
- **Expanding into dating** with "Crush" feature (anonymously signal interest in people from events you've both attended)
- **No public discovery** — invite-only model means they'll never compete for the "what's happening near me" space
- **Lesson for Clubd:** The social transparency of "who's going" is incredibly powerful. Partiful proved this for private events. Clubd should bring that same mechanic to public community events.

## Ideal Customer Profiles (ICPs)

### Consumer ICPs

#### ICP 1: The Active Social Seeker
- **Who:** 25–34, lives in OC/urban-suburban areas, health-conscious, social
- **Lifestyle:** Goes to 2-4 events/week (run club Tuesday, yoga Thursday, brunch pop-up Saturday, beach cleanup Sunday). Follows wellness influencers. Posts stories from events.
- **Discovery behavior:** Currently finds events through Instagram stories, friend texts, and studio email lists. Misses events constantly because discovery is fragmented.
- **Psychographic:** "I want to do MORE stuff but I never know what's happening until it's too late."
- **Why Clubd:** One place to see everything. Friends' activity surfaces new events. Never misses the seal release again.
- **Examples:** The 28-year-old in Newport Beach who does November Project at 6am, pilates at lunch, and wants to find the new popup dinner everyone's talking about.

#### ICP 2: The Wellness Curious Explorer
- **Who:** 22–30, newer to the area or looking to expand their social circle, interested in wellness/fitness but not yet "in the scene"
- **Lifestyle:** Goes to 1-2 events/month, wants to do more but feels intimidated by not knowing anyone. Browses Instagram longingly but doesn't pull the trigger.
- **Discovery behavior:** Searches "things to do in [city]" on Google. Scrolls Instagram explore page. Asks friends "what are you doing this weekend?"
- **Psychographic:** "I want to meet people and try new things but I don't want to show up alone to something where everyone already knows each other."
- **Why Clubd:** Seeing that 3 friends are "interested" in an event lowers the barrier. Social proof converts spectators into attendees.
- **Examples:** The 24-year-old who just moved to Irvine for work and wants to find her run club, her yoga studio, her Saturday morning crew.

#### ICP 3: The Connected Parent
- **Who:** 30–40, parent of young kids, suburban OC
- **Lifestyle:** Dad walk groups, mom & baby yoga, family-friendly outdoor events, kid-friendly brewery afternoons. Wants community but has limited time windows.
- **Discovery behavior:** Facebook groups (reluctantly), neighborhood apps (Nextdoor), other parents at school/daycare.
- **Psychographic:** "I used to have a social life. Now I need events that work around nap schedules and include strollers."
- **Why Clubd:** Filters for family-friendly events, sees which other parents are going, discovers the dad walk group that meets 3 blocks from their house.
- **Examples:** The 35-year-old dad in Costa Mesa who wants to find his tribe — other dads who run, surf, and bring their kids to the park on Saturday mornings.

#### ICP 4: The Weekend Experience Seeker
- **Who:** 26–38, couple or friend group, disposable income, values experiences over things
- **Lifestyle:** Popup dinners, wine tasting events, live music at breweries, outdoor movie nights, art walks, food festivals. Plans weekends around "what's the move?"
- **Discovery behavior:** Group texts, Instagram, Yelp events (rarely). One person in the group is usually the "planner."
- **Psychographic:** "We're always looking for cool things to do this weekend but keep defaulting to the same restaurants because we don't know what else is happening."
- **Why Clubd:** Curated weekend discovery. "Here's everything happening this Saturday within 15 miles." Share events directly to the group chat.
- **Examples:** The couple in Huntington Beach who want to find the secret supper club, the live jazz at the new brewery, the sunset paddleboard session.

### Host ICPs

#### Host ICP 1: The Fitness Instructor / Studio Owner
- **Who:** Runs a pilates studio, yoga practice, run club, or boot camp. 1-5 person team.
- **Pain:** "I spend more time on marketing than teaching. I'm posting on Instagram, managing a Linktree, sending emails, and using Google Forms for signups. My events would fill faster if more people knew about them."
- **Current stack:** Instagram + Linktree + Google Forms + Mailchimp + Canva + Venmo
- **Why Clubd:** Replaces 6 tools with one. Events auto-discoverable by nearby users. Attendee management built in. Payment processing included.
- **Revenue potential:** Clubd Pro subscriber ($29-99/mo) + booking commission on paid classes + promoted events

#### Host ICP 2: The Community Organizer
- **Who:** Leads a dad walk group, a book club, a beach cleanup crew, a nonprofit event series, or a social sports league. Does this as a passion project, not a business.
- **Pain:** "I organize events for free and can barely get the word out beyond my existing group. I'd love to grow but I don't have time for marketing."
- **Current stack:** Facebook group or group text + maybe an Instagram page
- **Why Clubd:** Free to list events. Clubd's discovery feed brings in new people organically. RSVP management saves time. The social graph brings friends-of-friends.
- **Revenue potential:** Free tier initially → converts to Clubd Pro as the group grows → may use marketing services for bigger events

#### Host ICP 3: The Local Business Host
- **Who:** Brewery, restaurant, coffee shop, or retail store that hosts occasional events (trivia nights, tasting events, popup chefs, live music, art shows).
- **Pain:** "We host events to drive foot traffic but only our regulars know about them. We post on Instagram but reach is terrible."
- **Current stack:** Instagram + in-store flyers + maybe Eventbrite for ticketed events
- **Why Clubd:** Reaches event-seeking consumers who aren't already following them. Built-in payment for ticketed events. Analytics on who's coming and converting.
- **Revenue potential:** Promoted events ($50-500/event) + booking commission + possible venue partnership ($199-999/mo)

## What Clubd Should Learn From Each Competitor

### From Fever: Learn These
1. **Build your own media network.** Fever's Secret Media Network (city-specific "what to do" social pages) is their most underrated asset. Clubd should consider launching "Clubd OC" / "Clubd LA" social accounts as editorial-style event discovery content that funnels to the platform.
2. **Data → original content flywheel.** Once Clubd has enough user behavior data, use it to identify demand gaps and either create events or recruit hosts to fill them. "We see 500 people searching for run clubs in Irvine but only 2 options — want to start one?"
3. **Design events for shareability.** Every event listed on Clubd should have a beautiful, shareable card optimized for Instagram stories. Make it dead simple for attendees to share "I'm going to this" and generate organic reach.

### From Sweatpals: Learn These
1. **Vertical-specific host tools matter.** Sweatpals built waiver signing because fitness hosts need it. Clubd should identify the specific tools each host vertical needs (waivers for fitness, dietary collection for food events, volunteer hour tracking for nonprofits).
2. **Ride the cultural wave hard.** Run clubs growing 216% YoY is a headline Clubd should be shouting. Position the platform at the center of the "third places" and "community fitness" cultural moments.
3. **City-by-city expansion works.** Sweatpals' city rollout (Miami, Austin, Chicago, NYC, LA) mirrors Clubd's go-to-market. Study which cities they struggled in and why.

### From Partiful: Learn These
1. **Frictionless RSVP is everything.** No app download required, text-based, one-tap. Clubd's guest experience (for people not yet on the platform) should be equally frictionless.
2. **The "who's going" transparency is a killer feature.** Seeing friends on the guest list converts undecided people. This should be Clubd's most prominent UI element on every event card.
3. **Playful, personality-driven design wins with 22-35.** Partiful's aesthetic (memes, GIFs, humor) made event planning feel fun, not utilitarian. Clubd should have personality — this isn't enterprise software.

## Strategic Recommendations

### 1. Own "Lifestyle Events" — Not "Everything"
Don't try to be Meetup (too broad) or Sweatpals (too narrow). Own the category between them: **lifestyle events for people who are active, social, and experience-oriented.** This means:
- Yes: Run clubs, yoga, pilates, popup dinners, brewery events, beach cleanups, art walks, dad groups, matcha meetups, live music in the park, farmers markets, charity events
- No: Tech meetups, corporate networking, professional conferences, political rallies, gaming events, academic seminars

### 2. Lead with Social Discovery, Not Search
Fever and Sweatpals are browse/search marketplaces. Clubd's #1 differentiator is that discovery is SOCIAL. The home screen should lead with "Your friends are going to..." and "People like you attended..." — not a category grid.

### 3. Start Hyper-Local, Then Expand
Launch in Orange County with 30-50 handpicked hosts across all lifestyle categories. Prove the social flywheel in one market before expanding. The OC audience (health-conscious, experience-seeking, social, 25-40) is the perfect testbed.

### 4. Build the Media Arm Early
Start "Clubd OC" Instagram/TikTok accounts immediately — even before the app launches. Curate "things to do this weekend in OC" content. Build audience → launch platform → funnel audience to app. This is Fever's playbook at city scale.

### 5. Make the Free Tier Unbeatable for Hosts
Sweatpals and Fever charge hosts. Clubd should make the free tier generous enough that every run club leader, yoga instructor, and community organizer lists their events on Clubd without hesitation. Monetize through Pro tools, commissions, and promotions — not by gatekeeping basic event listing.
